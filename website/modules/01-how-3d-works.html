Day 1: How 3D Works — Meshes, Materials & Lighting

Core insight: Everything in 3D — a spaceship, a coffee mug, a character — is just a collection of triangles with color and lighting math applied to each pixel. Once you understand that, the whole field clicks.

The Building Blocks: Vertices, Edges & Faces

Every 3D object is a mesh — a network of points in 3D space connected into polygons.

1
Vertex — a single point in 3D space with X, Y, Z coordinates (e.g. [1.0, 0.5, -2.0]).
2
Edge — a line connecting two vertices. Defines the wireframe skeleton of the object.
3
Face / Triangle — three vertices connected into a polygon. GPUs render triangles exclusively; quads (4-sided faces) are split before rendering.

Materials & PBR

A material tells the renderer how a surface looks under light. Modern 3D uses Physically Based Rendering (PBR) — simulating real-world optics with a few key properties:

  • Albedo — base color, what the surface looks like under pure white light
  • Roughness — 0 = mirror, 1 = chalk. Controls how sharp reflections are
  • Metalness — 0 = plastic/ceramic, 1 = bare metal. Changes how color and reflections behave
  • Normal map — a texture that fakes surface detail (bumps, scratches) without adding polygons

Lighting

Without lights, PBR materials render black. Three main light types:

  • Directional — parallel rays, like the sun. Set a direction, not a position
  • Point — emits equally in all directions from a point, like a light bulb
  • Spot — cone-shaped beam with falloff, like a flashlight
Ambient light is a flat base brightness added to everything in the scene — it prevents completely dark shadows. Use it sparingly; it flattens the appearance of depth.

UV Mapping

UV mapping is how 2D textures "wrap" onto 3D surfaces. U and V are the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the texture (like X and Y, but for image space). Every vertex also stores a UV coordinate telling the renderer which part of the texture image to sample at that point.

What is a vertex in 3D graphics?

  • A single pixel in a texture image
  • A point in 3D space with X, Y, Z coordinates
  • A polygon made of three points
  • A direction vector indicating surface orientation

In PBR materials, what does the roughness property control?

  • The base color of the surface
  • How bumpy the surface looks
  • How sharp or blurry reflections appear (0 = mirror, 1 = chalk)
  • Whether the surface behaves like a metal or plastic
